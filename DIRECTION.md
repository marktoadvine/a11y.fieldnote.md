# Direction: lightweight accessibility fieldnotes

AFS makes accessibility thinking easy to record, share, and carry into a project. Designers and developers can copy one file, write useful notes, and keep those notes beside a component through handoff and reuse. The same file is readable by people and software.

Version 0.3.0 implements this direction: a component identifier and a list of notes with stable IDs. Familiar fields such as topic, statement, question, reason, and references are optional. Custom topics and fields are welcome; preserve the meaning of shared fields so tools can recognize them.

Accessibility depends on the people, language, region, platform, framework, and implementation involved. Authors should document those nuances in their own terms and link relevant guidance. IDs identify notes; they do not establish checklist coverage or compliance.

Keep the format small. Configuration machinery and verification-result records are outside its scope. Prioritize a copyable starter, suggested questions, and realistic examples. Authoring needs no installation or mandatory tooling.

Success means someone can pick up the file, understand the accessibility choices and open questions, and use it in their project with little explanation.
